About

About & disclosure

Who runs this site, where the numbers come from, how it is paid for and how errors are corrected.

Who runs it

Underkeel is run by Dmitrii Luzin, an independent developer. I also run liquidation bots on Base, so I may profit from liquidations in the markets measured here. That is a conflict of interest, and I would rather you read it here than find it. You do not have to trust me: the method and the data are public (Method, Data), and every snapshot is a commit in a public repository (history of the data). Liquidations and bonuses can be rechecked on chain. Exit costs can be measured again the same way, but not replayed for a past snapshot: quotes, RFQ ones above all, hold only for the moment they are given.

What the site does not publish

Borrower addresses, my own positions, bots or strategies. It publishes market-wide measurements only.

Independence, not investment advice

Not affiliated with Morpho, Base, KyberSwap or Chainlink. Informational only. Nothing on this site is a recommendation to supply, borrow, liquidate or change a market's parameters. Exit costs are a snapshot: in a fast market they rise exactly when liquidations are needed.

Where this is discussed

On the Morpho forum, in the RFC thread "Collateral Transparency": the first exit costs against the bonus (my post 21, 13 Nov), this site (post 27, 17 Nov) and cbXRP/USDC on 8 Nov, minute by minute (post 32, 20 Nov).

Running since 14 Nov 2026: 47 snapshots, the longest gap between two of them 18 h. Full history: every snapshot as a commit.

Why "Underkeel"

Under-keel clearance is the water left between a ship's keel and the seabed. Underkeel measures the same margin for a lending market: what the liquidation bonus leaves room for, minus the cost of selling the collateral.

Money

Underkeel has no token and no sponsor. Its costs are a domain, free static hosting and the operator's time. If it receives a grant or fees from a protocol, a curator or any market participant, the source, the amount and the date will be listed here.

Paid work

One service: an exit-risk review of a curator's markets (Services). A review is private to the client and never changes the public numbers; a client cannot buy a different measurement of its market. Clients are named here only with their consent.

Visit counts

The site counts page views, how long a page stays visible, how far it is scrolled and which links are followed out, to the data files or to the contacts. With each view it keeps the page that linked here (site and path, no query) or, for a link we posted with a channel tag (?ref=x), only that tag, the country and the kind of device (desktop, phone, tablet). It sets no cookies and keeps no IP address or browser string: a visitor is a hash of the address and the browser mixed with a random key that is replaced every day, and the counts never join one day's visitors to another's. The collector deletes events after 60 days; for the counts, the operator's server keeps the same rows, with no IP address or browser string, for up to 400 days, and its backups for two weeks after that. The counter is a small script sending to the site's own collector on Cloudflare; with scripts off nothing is sent. Only the operator sees the counts.

Data sources and terms

  • Exit quotes: the KyberSwap Aggregator public API, best route on Base for each size, with and without RFQ quotes.
  • Markets, prices and positions: the Morpho API.
  • Liquidations: Morpho Blue events on Base, read from the chain.

Each source's terms apply to what is derived from it. The numbers are measurements of public data at the snapshot time; how each one is made is on the Method page, the files on Data.

Corrections

  • 23 Nov 2026: In the note "Two minutes when a 12.7% bonus was not enough in cbXRP/USDC on Base": The note said cbXRP traded 9.1% and 8.0% below Coinbase in the minutes starting at 21:56 and 21:57 UTC. That measure missed one cbXRP pool and did not read the swaps of another, which logs them in a different format; with both, the gaps are 9.1% and 7.9%. The 30 October gap stays at 3.5-4%.
  • 23 Nov 2026: In the note "Two minutes when a 12.7% bonus was not enough in cbXRP/USDC on Base": The note counted 140 standing positions with a median debt of $192. They were 140 liquidations of 115 positions; $192 is the median a liquidation repaid, and the median position owed $1,217.
  • 23 Nov 2026: In the note "Two minutes when a 12.7% bonus was not enough in cbXRP/USDC on Base": The note guessed that most of the liquidatable collateral at 21:56:04 UTC left through the auctions or repayment. Measured, small liquidations brought most of those positions back under their limit.
  • 16 Nov 2026: Until this date every clearance on the site was the liquidation bonus minus the exit cost. A liquidation breaks even earlier, at an exit cost of 1 - 1/(1 + bonus), so the published clearances were too high by 0.01 to 1.43 percentage points (the most at LLTV 62.5%: bonus 12.68%, break-even 11.25%). Corrected in Clearance v3; statuses on the snapshot of 16 Nov 12:23 UTC did not change.

Every correction is listed here with its date: what was shown, what is right, which snapshots and pages it touched, and when it was fixed. Published JSON files are not rewritten; the history stays in git (every snapshot as a commit).
