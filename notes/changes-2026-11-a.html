What changed - snapshots of 14-20 Nov 2026

USDe/USDC debt fell $94.7M in ten hours; its $1M exit held at 38%

From 14 to 20 November, USDe/USDC borrowers took 90.3M USDe out with their repayments and sold none of it in those transactions, SOL/USDC 62.5% gained pool depth, and cbLTC/USDC 62.5% lost a maker.

By Dmitrii Luzinpublished 21 Nov 20262 min readClearance v3

I run liquidation bots on Base and may profit from liquidations in the markets I measure. This note says nothing about my own positions or results. About & disclosure

From 15:00 UTC on 19 November, borrowers in USDe/USDC 91.5% repaid $94.7M of their $329.0M debt in under ten hours. The cost of selling $1M of USDe did not move: about 38% in the snapshots before the first repayment and after the last, against a break-even of 2.55%, the cost at which a liquidation pays nothing.

The lending side moved first. One lender, a Morpho vault, began large withdrawals 21 minutes before the first repayment and ended them three minutes after the final one, taking out $137.0M. I checked its own deposits by the hour: they stayed within half a percent, so the money most likely went to its other markets. The borrow rate more than tripled, to 17.1%, and peaked twice before easing.

I read the receipts of all 42 repayments by eight borrowers; in 36 the borrower also withdrew USDe collateral, 90.3M in all. None of the transactions logged a DEX swap or a Morpho flash loan, and all that USDe ended with the borrower or the account that sent the transaction. They paid in USDC and kept their USDe; in these transactions none of it went into a pool a liquidator would sell into.

No position in this market was liquidated in the whole review period.

SOL/USDC 62.5% gained pool depth. In the first snapshot a $1M sale of SOL came in under the break-even only because RFQ makers carried most of the route; on pools only, six times the break-even. The pools deepened on 17 November, and the sale is now under the break-even on pools only too. I have not looked at which pool.

cbLTC/USDC 62.5% is a small market, so a $200k sale is about a third of its debt. In the first two snapshots a maker the aggregator shows as pmm-19 quoted all of it. Early on 15 November, in the hours of the fall's low, it dropped out of the cbLTC and SOL routes. It was back in SOL by evening; I have not seen it in a cbLTC route since, and on pools only the sale now loses four-fifths of its value. Why SOL and not cbLTC?

What this does not show

The first snapshot came four hours before the low of the fall of 12-15 November. Its column in the table is not a quiet baseline, and the USDe debt had fallen by $10.9M before the repayments began.

Still, the SOL and cbLTC changes are not just the market calming down. The SOL pools deepened two days after the low, and the maker has stayed out of cbLTC for five days.

My snapshots, six hours apart, cannot see a pool that thinned and refilled between them, and Morpho's API gives the rate once an hour, so either peak might have been higher. I read only the repayment transactions, not what borrowers did with the USDe before or after.

Nor did the repayments make the USDe exit safer. Borrowers still owe $234.4M, interest included, and a $1M sale costs 38.37%.
