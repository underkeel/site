Method note - Clearance v3

A 12.68% bonus breaks even at an 11.25% exit cost

Until 16 November this site set the cost of selling seized collateral against the liquidation bonus. A liquidator breaks even at a lower cost: 11.25% when the bonus is 12.68%. This note is the correction.

By Dmitrii Luzinpublished 18 Nov 20264 min readClearance v3

I run liquidation bots on Base and may profit from liquidations in the markets I measure. This note says nothing about my own positions or results. About & disclosure

At 06:23 UTC on 17 November a $1M sale of cbXRP into USDC on Base cost 12.03%. The cbXRP/USDC 62.5% market on Morpho pays a 12.68% bonus, and until 16 November I would have shown that sale here with 0.65 points of room. The liquidation behind it loses money.

The line the sale has to stay under is 11.25%.

The bonus is paid on the debt, and the cost of the sale is paid on the collateral, which is the larger amount. A liquidator who repays $100 seizes $112.68 of collateral at the oracle price. Selling it at a cost c returns $112.68 x (1 - c), and that has to cover the $100. It does while c stays under 1 - 100/112.68, which is 11.25%.

I find it easier in dollars. The $1M of cbXRP is the collateral of a liquidation that repays $887.5k of debt, the sale returns $879.7k, and the liquidator is $7.8k short before gas.

In general the break-even exit cost is 1 - 1/(1 + bonus). Morpho sets the bonus from the LLTV, and below its 15% cap the break-even comes out as 0.3 x (1 - LLTV).1 That is the whole correction.

From the first version of the method, dated 13 November, I set the exit cost against the bonus itself, and the Method page said so in as many words. The rule was simple and wrong by the bonus times the break-even, which at the top tier is more than a point.

What changed on the site

I changed the rule with the snapshot of 14:46 UTC on 16 November (Clearance v3). Clearance is now the break-even exit cost minus the exit cost; before, it was the bonus minus the exit cost. The difference depends only on the market's LLTV and is the same at every sale size.

Ten of the 47 markets sit at the 62.5% tier, the only one where the old rule was off by more than a point.

Did it change what the site said about any market? I ran both rules over every snapshot the public repository keeps. A status moves only when an exit cost lands between the break-even and the bonus, and that is rare.

On the last snapshot under the old rule, at 12:23 UTC on 16 November, no market had such a cost. In the 8 snapshots published before the change it happened in 2 readings of 376, both at the 62.5% tier and both on 15 November: cbADA/USDC at $25k and cbXRP/USDC at $1M. Each read "RFQ-only" under the old rule and "loss" under the new one.

Since the change it has happened twice more, in the two latest snapshots, both times cbXRP/USDC at $1M.

The closest call is SOL/USDC 62.5%. A $1M sale costs 11.17%, which leaves 0.08 points; the old rule would have shown 1.51. Its quote moved by more than that between the last two snapshots, so I would not call it a margin.

I did not recompute past exit costs. The history table on each market page keeps them as published and sets every one against the break-even, the old snapshots included.

What this does not show

The break-even is still an upper bound on what a liquidator keeps. Gas comes off it, and so do the bids in the Chainlink SVR auctions: in the 30 days to 17 November, 93% of the bonus in Morpho liquidations on Base went through those auctions, and the winners paid back 81% of it as bids.

The exit cost is the route's own cost. If the oracle price lags the market in a fast move, a liquidator bears that gap too, and neither rule includes it. I don't know how wide that gap gets on Base when it matters, and a snapshot taken every six hours is the wrong instrument to find out.

Both rules assume the seized collateral is sold in the same transaction. A liquidator who holds it, or sells it over several blocks, faces a different number.

On the same snapshot the cbXRP/USDC 62.5% page puts that $1M sale 0.78 points past the break-even, and the market's status reads "loss from $1M".

  1. Below the cap 1/(1 + bonus) = 0.3 x LLTV + 0.7, and 1 minus that is 0.3 x (1 - LLTV). The 15% cap applies below an LLTV of about 56.5%, where the break-even is 13.04%; no market on the site sits there. back
