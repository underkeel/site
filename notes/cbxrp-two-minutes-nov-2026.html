Event note - cbXRP/USDC 62.5% - Base - event 8 Nov 2026 21:56 UTC

Two minutes when a 12.7% bonus was not enough in cbXRP/USDC on Base

On 8 November the cbXRP pools on Base gave way under sales of seized collateral, and positions in Morpho's cbXRP/USDC 62.5% market stood liquidatable for up to 80 seconds. No bad debt followed.

By Dmitrii Luzinpublished 17 Nov 2026updated 23 Nov 20264 min readClearance v3

I run liquidation bots on Base and may profit from liquidations in the markets I measure. This note says nothing about my own positions or results. About & disclosure

At 21:53 UTC on 8 November cbXRP traded 0.2% below Coinbase in the pools on Base, and three minutes later 9.1% below. In the ten seconds when the pools fell fastest, 89% of the cbXRP sold into them came from transactions that also liquidated a position in Morpho's cbXRP/USDC 62.5% market. The liquidations made their own exit expensive: in all three blocks I read over the next minute, selling even $5k of cbXRP cost more than the bonus covers.

In the two minutes from 21:56 to 21:58 UTC, 140 liquidations in this market came well after the oracle step that made their positions liquidatable. They waited up to 80 seconds. On 30 October cbXRP also slipped below Coinbase on Base, by 3.5-4%, and every liquidation outside auctions was taken in the block where it appeared or in the next one.1

I found no bad debt in any of the 404 liquidations in this market that day, which repaid $1.58M of debt, so lenders lost nothing.2 The cushion is wide here: a position becomes liquidatable while its collateral is still worth 1.6 times the debt at the oracle price.

What a sale cost

A bonus is only money once the collateral is sold. This market sits in the top bonus tier of the markets I track. A liquidator who repays $100 seizes $112.68 of cbXRP at the oracle price, and breaks even if selling it loses 11.25% of its value.3 Lose more and the liquidation costs money, however generous the bonus looks.

cbXRP is Coinbase's wrapped XRP, and one cbXRP redeems for one XRP there, which is why Coinbase is the price to compare with. A liquidator who wants out in the same transaction has to sell into the pools on Base. So I asked those pools what a sale would have returned at the end of blocks that night.

Read the $1k column against the break-even. In the first block a small sale still kept a quarter of the bonus. Twenty-six seconds later it kept about a twentieth, and half a minute after that it lost money.

The liquidations that waited were small. The median one repaid $192 of debt, about $24 of bonus before the sale and gas, and together the 140 paid $9,196 of bonus. For a liquidation that size the $1k column is the one that counts.

The 140 liquidations fell on 115 of the 131 positions in the table's first row. Between that row and the third, liquidations repaid $77k, under a tenth of what those positions owed, and that brought most of them back under their limit: the liquidatable column fell from $1.15M to $43k with the oracle price unchanged.

What opened the gap

The pools held through the first two waves of liquidations.4 A minute after the second, a $1k sale still cost under 1.5% against the oracle, and in the transactions of either wave no more than $125 of cbXRP went into the pools.

I split the selling into the pools by whether the sale sat in a transaction that also liquidated a position in this market. In the seconds when the pools fell fastest, most of it did.

The last ten seconds in the table are where the cost of a $1k sale quadrupled. In the blocks of those seconds I see one pattern again and again: a liquidation, and in the same transaction a sale of the seized cbXRP into the pools.

What this does not show

My 9.1% is coarse: a minute of pool swaps against a Coinbase minute candle, while the price kept moving inside the minute. Against the oracle, the pools went from 7.3% below to 11.1% below between Table 1's first and third rows. My split of the selling is coarse too: it sees only the transaction a sale sits in. Seized collateral sold a block later or on another venue is outside the 45%, so the share might be higher. Arbitrage between pools is inside the $413k, so it might be lower.

My quotes use on-chain pools only. A market maker quoting off-chain may have paid more that night, and those quotes cannot be replayed afterwards. Makers carry most of this exit on a normal day: in my snapshot of 17 November a $1M cbXRP sale cost 12.0% with them and 61.6% without.

Is this rare? I think so, but this is the only episode of standing positions I have taken apart. I see when the liquidations came, not why they waited.

By 21:58 nothing was left standing. The bonus was 12.68% the whole time.

Corrections

Corrections

  • 23 Nov 2026: The note said cbXRP traded 9.1% and 8.0% below Coinbase in the minutes starting at 21:56 and 21:57 UTC. That measure missed one cbXRP pool and did not read the swaps of another, which logs them in a different format; with both, the gaps are 9.1% and 7.9%. The 30 October gap stays at 3.5-4%.
  • 23 Nov 2026: The note guessed that most of the liquidatable collateral at 21:56:04 UTC left through the auctions or repayment. Measured, small liquidations brought most of those positions back under their limit.
  • 23 Nov 2026: The note counted 140 standing positions with a median debt of $192. They were 140 liquidations of 115 positions; $192 is the median a liquidation repaid, and the median position owed $1,217.
