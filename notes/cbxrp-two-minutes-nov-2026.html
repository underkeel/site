Event note - cbXRP/USDC 62.5% - event 8 Nov 2026 21:56 UTC

Two minutes when a 12.7% bonus was not enough

On 8 November cbXRP traded 8-9% below Coinbase on Base. In the cbXRP/USDC 62.5% market on Morpho, 140 liquidations waited between 4.6 and 80 seconds for a taker.

By Dmitrii Luzinpublished 17 Nov 20264 min readClearance v3

I run liquidation bots on Base and may profit from liquidations in the markets I measure. This note says nothing about my own positions or results. About & disclosure

On 8 November, in the minute after 21:56 UTC, cbXRP on Base exchanges traded 9.1% below XRP on Coinbase, and in the next minute 8.0% below. During those two minutes 140 positions in the cbXRP/USDC 62.5% market on Morpho stayed liquidatable for anything between 4.6 and 80 seconds before somebody took them.

A block on Base takes 0.2 seconds, so the slowest of them waited through 401 blocks.

I expected the opposite. This market sits in the top bonus tier of the markets I track: a liquidator who repays $100 of debt seizes $112.68 of cbXRP at the oracle price, and liquidations that pay like that do not usually wait. The previous time cbXRP slipped below Coinbase on Base, on 30 October, the gap was smaller, 3.5-4%, and every one of the 259 liquidations outside auctions was taken in the block where it appeared or in the next one.1

What a sale cost

A bonus is only money once the collateral is sold. The arithmetic is short: seize $112.68, repay $100, and the liquidator breaks even if selling the cbXRP loses 11.25% of its value.2 Lose less and there is a profit. Lose more and the liquidation costs money, however generous the bonus looks on paper.

cbXRP is Coinbase's wrapped XRP, and one cbXRP redeems for one XRP there, which is why Coinbase is the price to compare with. A liquidator who wants out in the same transaction cannot wait for that and has to sell into the pools on Base. So I rebuilt the state of Base at the end of five blocks that night and asked those pools what a sale would have returned.

Read the $1k column against that 11.25%. In the first block a small sale still kept a quarter of the bonus. Twenty-six seconds later it kept about a twentieth, and half a minute after that it lost money.

Anything from $5k up lost money in all three blocks.

For scale, in the snapshot of 17 November the same $25k sale through on-chain pools cost 0.22%.

Liquidators were watching all the while: 68 different senders ended up taking those 140 positions. My reading is that they were waiting for a sale that paid. I can only see when they acted and not why, so that part is my guess.

The positions left standing were small. Their median debt was $192, which is about $24 of bonus before the sale and before gas, and together they carried $9,196 of bonus.

The large positions did not stand. In the first block of the table there was $1.15M of liquidatable collateral in this market, and under a minute later there was $43k.

I did not trace where they went. The liquidations outside auctions that I count here add up to $38,994 of bonus for the whole day, against $129k available at that one block, so most of it left some other way: through the auctions attached to oracle updates, or because borrowers repaid in time.

One position I followed by hand became liquidatable when the market's oracle stepped down by 1.8%. It was taken 309 blocks later, which is 62 seconds.

What this does not show

The 8-9% is coarse. It is the volume-weighted price of pool swaps within a minute against a Coinbase candle, and inside the minute the price kept moving. Measured against the oracle, the pools went from 7.3% below to 11.1% below in under a minute.

My quotes use on-chain pools only. A market maker quoting off-chain may have paid more that night, and those quotes cannot be replayed afterwards. It matters, because makers carry most of this exit on a normal day: in the snapshot of 17 November a $1M cbXRP sale cost 12.0% with them and 61.6% without.

Is this rare? I think so, but I would not lean on it: this is the only episode of standing positions I have taken apart.

By 21:58 the pools were back to 5.3% below the oracle and nothing was left standing. Seven minutes after that, a $25k sale cost 4.8%. The bonus was 12.68% the whole time.

  1. Blocks on Base took 2 seconds then, so "the next block" meant up to 4 seconds. "Outside auctions" means liquidations that did not go through the auctions attached to oracle price updates.   2. 1 - 100 / 112.68 = 11.25%; for any bonus the break-even exit cost is 1 - 1/(1 + bonus). The site's clearance figure is this break-even minus the exit cost. Until 16 November it was the bonus minus the exit cost, which overstated the room: a rounding matter at a 2.6% bonus, 1.4 points at this one. back
