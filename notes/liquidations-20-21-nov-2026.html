Event note - Morpho on Base - 20-21 Nov 2026 - event 20 Nov 2026 14:00 UTC

$91.7M liquidated on Morpho on Base as BTC fell 10.8%

cbBTC carried nine-tenths of it, and Base's pools for it stayed deep. In two markets where the site shows a sale breaking, the collateral seized added up to more than that sale, but in pieces.

By Dmitrii Luzinpublished 24 Nov 20264 min readClearance v3

I run liquidation bots on Base and may profit from liquidations in the markets I measure. This note says nothing about my own positions or results. About & disclosure

Liquidations on Morpho Blue on Base repaid $91.7M of debt on Friday and Saturday, 20 and 21 November, two-fifths of all they repaid in the month to 22 November. Bad debt written off against lenders came to less than a cent. I leave out five unlisted markets created during the fall, which look like tests.1

For a lender or a curator, the two days show that cbBTC's exit on Base held. Where the site shows a sale breaking, the fall did not test the size at which it breaks.

BTC on Coinbase fell 10.8% from 14:00 UTC on Friday to its low at 06:17 UTC on Saturday. Liquidations came in three bursts, and the last, in the two hours from 03:00 UTC on Saturday, repaid $42.7M, nearly half of the two days' debt. I went through Saturday morning's blocks. That burst was over before BTC's low, though while it ran the price came within 0.7% of it. For the next whole hour not one Morpho position on Base was liquidated. The hour of the low brought $1.4M more. Most of what that price made liquidatable had likely gone already.

cbBTC/USDC 86%, the site's largest market, carried $83.7M of the debt.2

For cbBTC the cost of a sale stayed far below the 4.20% at which a liquidation here stops paying.3 The site prices it every six hours, and in none of its ten snapshots from 06:22 UTC on Friday to 12:23 UTC on Sunday did a $1M sale cost more than 0.5%.

The site took none during the last burst, so for those hours I read Base's pools directly, through the best single path and without the aggregator the site uses. I read eight blocks across those two hours, chosen by the clock and by BTC's low, without regard to where the liquidations fell. A $1M sale lost at most 1.1% against a small one. At the snapshot blocks the same reading came out close to the site's or above it.4

The snapshots also count cbBTC/USDC collateral in positions within about 9% of liquidation, whichever positions sit there at the time. The highest of the ten was $213.1M, about four hours into the fall, and the first one after the low still showed $188.3M. Fifteen single liquidations in this market seized more than the largest sale the site prices.

Bad debt, the part of a loan its collateral no longer covers, falls on the market's lenders when a liquidation takes the last of the collateral. In markets lending USDC, that happened twice, both in cbBTC/USDC, for a fraction of a cent each. The previous week's fall wrote off $6.52 in markets lending USDC.

Most of its borrowing is in USDe/USDC 91.5%, two dollar-pegged tokens, where nothing was liquidated.

What this does not show

The site's break size is for one sale. Small sales within an hour cost about what one sale of their sum would if the pools do not refill between them, and less if they do; I have not measured which it was here. Nor did I look at what liquidators did with the collateral, or at how far the price on Base left the oracle between the blocks I read.

  1. All five were created between 01:50 and 02:27 UTC on 21 November. One lends DAI against ETH, and its oracle put ETH at hundreds of thousands of dollars when the loan was taken; of a $2.32 loan there, $2.30 was written off. The other four lend a token named USD against one named Collateral, and three show the same supply in Morpho's API, 977,218.55 units. They wrote off about 478k units and repaid about 1.24M, which are not in the $91.7M. back
  2. Chainlink SVR auctions carried 92.7% of the two days' debt and 93.3% of the month's. back
  3. Morpho's liquidation factor at an 86% LLTV is 1 / (0.3 x 0.86 + 0.7): repaying $100 seizes $104.38 of cbBTC at the oracle price, and the sale stops paying at a cost of 1 - 100 / 104.38 = 4.20%. back
  4. I take the loss on $1M against a $1k sale through the same pools. At the four snapshot blocks of 20-21 November it gave 0.26%, 0.74%, 0.16% and 0.46%, against the site's 0.20%, 0.42%, 0.14% and 0.47%. Mine does not split the sale; the site's figure is KyberSwap's route over all sources. back
  5. On Coinbase, from 14:00 UTC on 20 November to the lowest hourly low before 12:00 UTC on 21 November, XRP lost 9.3% and ZEC 16.8%, against 10.8% for BTC. back
