Market note - USDe/USDC 91.5%

A $1M USDe sale on Base costs 37.7% against a 2.55% break-even

USDe/USDC 91.5% is the second largest Morpho market on Base, with $329M borrowed. On 17 November a $200k sale of USDe cost 0.10%, and a $1M sale cost 37.7%.

By Dmitrii Luzinpublished 17 Nov 20263 min readClearance v3

I run liquidation bots on Base and may profit from liquidations in the markets I measure. This note says nothing about my own positions or results. About & disclosure

Selling $200k of USDe into USDC on Base cost 0.10% at 06:23 UTC on 17 November. Selling $1M cost 37.7%. A liquidation in the USDe/USDC 91.5% market on Morpho pays only while the sale costs less than 2.55%.

That market has $329M borrowed, the second largest of those I track on Base.1

The bonus at this LLTV is 2.62%, so the arithmetic is short. A liquidator who repays $974.5k of debt seizes $1M of USDe, and the quote says that USDe sells for $623k.

The liquidation would come out $352k short.

The same trade at $200k leaves about $4.9k before gas. Somewhere between those two sizes the pools run out, and since I measure three sizes and nothing in between, I can't say where.

Would anyone need to sell $1M of USDe in one go? In the 30 days to 17 November nobody had to: there was not a single liquidation in this pair on Morpho Base, against 13,491 across all pairs.

Positions sit close to the line all the same: $367M of collateral is in positions with a health factor below 1.1. In a pegged pair this is normal. USDe and USDC both track the dollar, so the health factor follows the peg and not a market price.

Where the sale goes

Up to $200k the aggregator sends the whole sale to Uniswap v3. At $1M it sends about a third to KyberSwap limit orders and nearly all the rest to Uniswap v3.

When the best route uses off-chain orders, I also ask for the quote without RFQ makers and limit orders, which leaves the on-chain pools alone. At $1M that quote came back at 37.7% as well. The off-chain orders change where the USDe goes and leave the price where it is.

Then I looked at the history, which the market page keeps: 12 snapshots so far, over 57 hours.

The $1M sale never left a band a tenth of a point wide.

For comparison, a $1M sale of cbXRP, where RFQ makers carry much of the route, cost anywhere from 12.0% to 57.4% across the same snapshots. Here even the quote without limit orders moved only once, to 68.5% on 16 November, and otherwise stayed within a few hundredths of a point of the full quote.

I read the steadiness as a fixed amount of USDC on the other side of the pools, which a $1M sale uses up the same way every time. I have not looked inside the pools, so that part is a guess.

What this does not show

I measure one liquidation sold in one transaction. A position of $1M would in practice be liquidated in parts across blocks, and at $200k a part the sale clears with room to spare. How fast the pools refill between the parts is the number that would matter then, and a snapshot every six hours cannot see it.

The quotes come from KyberSwap's aggregator on Base. A sale on a centralized exchange, or a redemption of USDe with its issuer, is not measured, and a liquidator who can wait for either faces a different cost.

Each snapshot is one quote per size. I don't know what the $200k sale would cost on a day when USDe trades away from the dollar, and these snapshots can't tell me.

The bonus on a $1M liquidation here is $25.5k, and in each of the 12 quotes the sale behind it lost about $377k.
