Event note - nine Morpho markets on Base - event 12 Nov 2026 00:00 UTC

Borrowers made 60% of the debt in reach safe before the November low

Between 12 and 15 November, the Morpho markets on Base lending USDC at the lowest LLTV, 62.5%, had 9.0% of their debt liquidated. Those at 86% had 5.2%.

By Dmitrii Luzinpublished 19 Nov 20264 min readClearance v3

I run liquidation bots on Base and may profit from liquidations in the markets I measure. This note says nothing about my own positions or results. About & disclosure

In the four days from 12 to 15 November, the nine Morpho markets on Base that carried nearly all the liquidations had $241.9M of debt in reach of the fall. Liquidations took 40% of it.1

The owners of the other $145.0M repaid or added collateral in time; only $1,560 was left over, as dust from partial liquidations. To check they were in time, I read each position at the end of the block where its market's oracle hit its low: every one was healthy or already paid off.

In reach means the fall was enough to liquidate a position nobody touched. BTC fell 16.2% on Coinbase and the cbBTC/USDC oracle with it, so in that market a position that would have been liquidated after a 10% drop was in reach, and one that needed 20% was not. I read every position at midnight on the first day and followed it to the end of the fourth.

What I noticed first, going through the blocks, is how bunched the liquidations were. On Morpho Base only about one block in 2,500 had a liquidation in those four days, and the busiest, with 170, came almost two days before the low. Nearly every position in reach that was taken went in a single liquidation. Was anything left standing at the low? Nothing of size. At the end of each market's low block, the positions still over the line owed $44 between them.

Counted in positions rather than dollars, the split turns over.

Liquidations took two in three positions in reach. The median position they took owed $164 at midnight; the median position whose owner acted owed $5,868.

Oracle fall: the lowest price implied by a liquidation of $100 or more, against the price at the start.

Row by row, liquidations took between a fifth and a half of the debt in reach; in cbBTC/USDC, which holds most of it, 41%. Almost everything they took in the nine markets came from positions that were in reach at midnight.2

More liquidated at the lowest LLTV

The markets that ask for the most collateral per dollar, at 62.5% LLTV, had the largest share of their debt liquidated: 9.0% of what was borrowed at the start, against 5.2% at 86%.3 cbXRP/USDC holds most of that tier's debt and shows one reason for the gap.

XRP fell less than BTC, and yet the fall reached about a quarter of that market's debt against an eighth of cbBTC/USDC's, because the debt stood closer to the line. At midnight the median dollar of cbXRP debt was 24.9% from it, and of cbBTC debt 32.4%. A lower LLTV keeps more collateral behind each dollar lent, but cbXRP borrowers sat closer to their limit. I don't know why.

Without cbXRP/USDC, the tier still had 8.0% liquidated. Most of the rest of its collateral fell further than BTC: SOL, JitoSOL, DOGE and ADA by 17.5-19.1%.

What this does not show

"Repaid or added collateral" means a Repay or SupplyCollateral in the owner's name in the four days. An owner who crossed the line before the low and paid back before anyone liquidated them would count as in time. A repayment by an automated tool that the borrower set up in advance counts as the borrower's: on-chain they look the same.

A liquidation also took part of some of those positions, and what was left of them may have been healthy at the low because of it rather than the owner. They held $0.73M of the $145.0M.

The low I use is the lowest oracle price implied by a liquidation of $100 or more, not a reading of the oracle at every block. A lower print between liquidations would put more debt in reach, though I think not much: it would have produced liquidations of its own.

This is the first fall I have measured this way, so I have nothing to set the 40% against. For a lender, the four days cost $6.52 of bad debt across every market lending USDC on Morpho Base.
